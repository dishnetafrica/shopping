# CloudBSS v4.38 — the bot stops asserting things it does not know

**Date:** 2026-08-05 · **Zip:** GITHUB-UPLOAD-cloudbss-v4.38.zip (cumulative — supersedes v4.37; 190 files) · **Migration: YES** — `php artisan migrate` · **Delta: 5 files.**

From the Shop 31 conversation on 04 Aug.

## "Zyad shop is outside our free-delivery area"

We had no idea where Zyad Shop was. It is the customer's own shop name — they were answering "which delivery area?" the way a shopkeeper does, with the name of their shop.

The delivery check had exactly two branches: text contains one of your 13 free-delivery town names → free; **everything else** → "outside our free-delivery area". A shop name, a person's name, a typo, an emoji, the word "yes" — all of them came back to the customer as a statement of fact about your coverage, printed directly beneath a priced quotation, which makes it read as a commitment. Zyad Shop may be two streets from the store.

**Unknown is now its own answer**, and it asks instead of asserting:

> 📍 Which town or area is *Zyad shop* in? Delivery inside Juba and its markets is free; for anywhere else our team confirms the transport cost, or you can collect from Juba.

No price claimed, no distance implied, nothing refused. A town you deliver free to still says free; Wau and Malakal still get the transport line with the pickup option. Only the third case changed — and the third case was every unrecognised string.

**The matcher was also promising free delivery to nowhere.** It asked whether a free-town name appeared *anywhere inside* the customer's text, so "Custom" matched inside "**custom**er" and "Hai" matched inside "Shang**hai**" — both were told delivery is free. Matching is on whole words now. "Custom Market" still works.

Settings gains **Other towns we know** (`known_delivery_towns`), defaulting to the South Sudan towns, the Uganda towns and the regional cities. A destination in neither list is what triggers the question. Add to it whenever a customer names somewhere real that the bot had to ask about.

## The second quotation never existed

Look at the transcript: `ORD-Q260804-0LKF prepared` appears twice, sixty seconds apart, same number, with a delivery sentence in the second that is not in any template — and the pickup option quietly missing.

No code path can produce that. A genuine second capture at the same total hits the duplicate check and says *"already with our team"*; a genuine new one mints a new number. **What happened is that the model paraphrased its own earlier message out of the chat history**, reference number and all. Nothing stripped a real `ORD-Q…` out of the model's prose, so a customer was shown a document that had not been prepared.

The bot's own wording is now scrubbed of any quotation claim before our block is appended — both a bare reference and a whole line announcing one was prepared. The numbered block underneath is generated by us and is the only thing entitled to say a quotation exists.

## A quotation number now means one quotation

`quote_no` carried an index and a comment reading *"unique in practice"*. It is minted from four random characters inside a per-day namespace. A comment is not a constraint; this migration is. Any existing collisions are **renamed, not deleted** — a duplicate is still somebody's order — and the oldest row keeps the original number.

## Not fixed, and worth knowing

**The address is still thrown away.** `quotations.delivery_address` exists and the bot never writes it; `customer_profiles` has `address` and `city` and the bot never writes those either. "Zyad shop" survives only in a JSON blob on the quotation row, so your staff open the portal and see no delivery address, and next time this customer messages, the bot asks again. That is the next thing to do here.

**"Confirming your order now!" is still in the prompt.** The system prompt gives that exact sentence as its example, while the template printed immediately below says *"Our team will confirm availability & prices"*. The prompt was written before quote-first mode. It contradicts itself in one message, by design, and untangling it means rewriting the order protocol rather than patching a string.

**That mayonnaise has `Pack Size = 1`** despite its name saying 12 Jars/Carton, with `Unit` = "Box" and `Size` = "Carton". So the catalogue tells the model "(1/unit)" for a product whose name says twelve. It did not break this order — $47 is the carton price — but the customer was never told they are getting 12 jars, and stock will deduct wrong. It is also one of the 8 products whose cost is recorded in UGX (141,740 = $38.78, a 17.5% margin on $47).

**A real `delivery_zones` table exists** — match keywords, coordinates, radius, per-zone fees — and your scripted bot uses it. The AI bot still carries its own weaker copy of the idea. Worth consolidating when you next touch delivery.

## After you deploy

```
php artisan migrate
```

Then open Settings → the delivery section and look at **Other towns we know**. Anything a customer might name that is not on either list will make the bot ask rather than assume.

Each was caught.
