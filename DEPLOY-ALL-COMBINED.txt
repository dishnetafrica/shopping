6) CASHBOOK SUPPLIER PICKER + "SUPPLIER PAYMENT (ON ACCOUNT)"
==============================================================================
WHY: paying a supplier "on account" is settling what you already owe them — NOT buying
goods again. Booking it as a Purchase double-counts the items. Your books track supplier
debt as a running balance: owed = (credit purchases) + (opening) − (supplier payments).
A payment simply reduces that balance — that IS the automatic "adjust against items"; there
is no per-item matching to do.

WHAT'S NEW (record form + Edit modal on /panel/trade-cashbook):
  - A "Supplier" dropdown (loads your suppliers). Tag any purchase/payment to a supplier;
    the supplier now shows in the ledger's Particulars ("Purchase · Nira bhai").
  - A new category "Supplier payment (on account)". When you pick it on a Money-out and
    choose a supplier, the entry is booked as a SUPPLIER PAYMENT (stage=supplier_payment)
    that reduces "Suppliers We Owe" for that supplier — instead of a new purchase.
  - When a supplier is selected for a purchase/payment, a hint shows how much you currently
    owe them.
  - In the Edit modal you can now attach a supplier to an existing entry, and tick
    "Payment on supplier account (reduces what we owe)" to reclassify a mis-booked purchase
    into a supplier payment (or untick to turn it back). Owner/manager only; audited;
    period-lock guarded.

BEST PRACTICE (what to advise staff):
  - Record the goods ONCE, as a credit Purchase (New Purchase screen -> items + supplier ->
    Credit). That raises what you owe the supplier.
  - When you hand the supplier cash, record it as "Supplier payment (on account)" against that
    supplier. It nets against their balance automatically. Don't also record it as a Purchase.
  - If you never recorded the goods separately and a cash Purchase entry is your only record
    of the cost, leave it as a Purchase and just tag the supplier.

FILES (backend TradeCashbookController.php: store/update/data; frontend trade_cashbook.html)
No migration — supplier_id/supplier_name already exist on the order_costs table.

VERIFY (feature 6)
  - Cashbook -> Money out -> category "Supplier payment (on account)" -> pick a supplier ->

  - Edit an existing wrong Purchase (e.g. PUR-0094) -> choose the supplier -> tick "Payment on
    supplier account" -> Save. It becomes a supplier payment and the owed balance corrects.

7) CONSIGNMENT — one AWB, many orders (Kampala → Juba daily consolidation)   [PHASE 1]
==============================================================================
THE PROBLEM: South Sudan customers order individually, but you can't ship per order. Every
day Jayesh consolidates the ready orders into one load, sends it to Asish in Juba under a
single AWB, and Asish distributes to each customer. Until now a shipment could hold only ONE
order. Phase 1 lets one shipment (a "consignment"/AWB) carry MANY customer orders.

NEW SCREEN  /panel/consignment   (mobile-first; works for Jayesh and Asish on phones)
  Jayesh (Kampala):
    - "+ New consignment" -> creates a Kampala→Juba AWB (transporter, bus, Juba agent).
    - Open it -> "Add ready orders" -> search and add each customer order going today.
    - Advance custody with one tap: Dispatch -> Transporter received -> Bus departed ->
      Mark arrived in Juba. (Reuses the existing shipment custody engine.)
  Asish (Juba), on arrival:
    - Opens the same consignment -> sees the full manifest (every order + customer + items).
    - Taps "Delivered" on each order as he hands it out (or "Returned"). Delivering an order
      sets that customer order to Delivered (fires the customer WhatsApp) and records it.

HOW IT FITS: a consignment IS a shipment, so the transporter/agent token pages, custody
ledger, box counts and dashboard all still apply. Orders keep their identity end-to-end.
An order MAY sit on more than one consignment (an order split across two buses is allowed).
